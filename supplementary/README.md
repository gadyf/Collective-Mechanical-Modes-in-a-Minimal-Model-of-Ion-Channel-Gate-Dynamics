## Captions

**Figure S1.** The two zero-frequency modes (modes 1 and 2) of the unstressed spring network.
- Grey: equilibrium configuration, with the springs (dashed) and the fixed anchoring points (grey circles).
- Coloured solid and dotted lines: the rods displaced in the two opposite directions along each mode. The arrows show the displacement of the rod ends for the solid configuration.
- Blue and orange: long inner rods (pore walls); green and red: short outer rods.

The figure shows linear mode shapes, with displacements exaggerated for visibility (largest rod-end displacement 3.3 Å in mode 1 and 3.6 Å in mode 2). The two modes have the same (zero) frequency, so any combination of them is also a zero mode; both are mirror-antisymmetric. The basis shown separates the motion of the pore walls from that of the outer rods:
- **Zero mode 1:** all of the (mass-weighted) motion is in the pore walls. They slide along their axes in opposite directions, with a smaller sideways shift and tilt in the same sense. This changes the asymmetric shift Δy but not the pore width.
- **Zero mode 2:** 99% of the motion is in the outer rods, which slide axially in opposite directions and tilt. The pore width is unchanged, and Δy changes by less than 5% of its change in mode 1.

Neither motion has a restoring force in the harmonic model.

**Animations S1 and S2.** Zero modes 1 and 2 (see Figure S1). The rods are moved one way along the mode, from one end of the displayed range to the other, and the loop then restarts. A zero mode has no restoring force and no frequency, so it is not shown as an oscillation, and the speed of the animation has no physical meaning. Grey: equilibrium configuration. Same amplitude as Figure S1.

**Animations S3–S12.** Normal modes 3–12 (Animation S*n* shows mode *n*), over one period of oscillation. Frequencies are given in Table 1 of the paper. Blue and orange: long inner rods (pore walls); green and red: short outer rods. All modes are shown with the same normalised (mass-weighted) amplitude, exaggerated for visibility. The physical rms amplitudes at 310 K are given in Table 1 and Table S2. Because the thermal amplitude scales as 1/ω, the soft modes 3 and 4 fluctuate much more than they appear to here. Axes in nm.

## How the files were made

- **Animations S3–S12** are the GIFs written by the notebook (`rods_animation_mode_N_one_period.gif`), renamed. `scripts/verify_animation_modes.py` measures the rod positions from the GIF pixels and confirms that each one shows its own mode (|cos| = 0.97–1.00 with the computed eigenvector; next-best mode ≤ 0.21).
- **MP4 files** are frame-by-frame conversions of the GIFs at 50 frames/s, with the original timing.
- **Zero modes (Fig. S1, Animations S1–S2)** are computed as the null space of the mass-weighted spring compatibility matrix; the residual spring-length change is ≈3×10⁻¹⁶. The basis is the pair of eigenvectors of the inner-rod projector within the null space. They are drawn like the notebook animations, at mass-weighted amplitude 0.7, which is 3.5 times the amplitude of Animations S3–S12. They show the linear (harmonic) mode shape. In the full nonlinear network the motion stays exactly free of spring stretching only up to about 1–1.5 Å of rod-end displacement (`scripts/zero_mode_finite.py`), so the larger displacement drawn here is for visibility only.
